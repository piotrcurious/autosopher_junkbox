Yes. I think an autosopher-based meta-transformer that attempts to solve the Chinese room problem through persistent, testable, transferable knowledge could be a much more revealing demonstration than another benchmark where an LLM answers difficult questions.

The key is to make the system demonstrate something that a conventional conversational model does not inherently provide: it acquires a concept through interaction, builds an explicit representation of what it has learned, tests that representation against new observations, corrects it when necessary, and transfers the resulting knowledge to a sister model that did not experience the original learning process.

The sister model should not merely receive a transcript or a summary. It should receive a working knowledge structure, together with the procedures and evidence needed to reconstruct, test and extend the concept.

That gives us a falsifiable experiment. We would not have to settle the philosophical question of machine consciousness to demonstrate a substantial architectural advance. We could test whether the system develops operationally grounded, self-correcting knowledge that survives across sessions and across different models.

I would design the demo around three challenges: learning a concept, demonstrating understanding through novel applications, and transferring that capability to another system.

### The demo: a meta-transformer that learns a world, not merely a language

I would give the system a small simulated world with unfamiliar objects, arbitrary names and unknown causal rules. The vocabulary and rules would be generated afresh for each experiment, preventing the foundation model from succeeding simply through its pretrained knowledge.

Stage 1

### Model A: acquire grounded knowledge

A transformer explores the simulated world. It sees objects, performs actions, observes results, makes predictions and receives feedback. It must discover what its symbols refer to and how the world behaves.

Stage 2

### Autosopher: consolidate the discovery

The system stores a typed concept graph, observed examples, causal relationships, competing hypotheses, confidence, counterexamples and executable tests. It records not just what it believes, but the evidence and procedures supporting those beliefs.

Stage 3

### Model B: inherit knowledge without inheriting the experience

A separately instantiated sister model receives the structured knowledge package, not Model A's conversation history or model weights. It must retrieve, test and apply the acquired concepts to new combinations of objects and situations.

Stage 4

### Reciprocal learning

Model B encounters a counterexample, proposes a correction and validates it against new observations. The correction enters a versioned knowledge package, which Model A can subsequently import and use.

The final stage is especially important. It tests whether the two systems participate in a shared process of knowledge development rather than merely passing static data back and forth.

### What would make this a serious test?

I would require five measurable results.

| Test           | Evidence of success                                                                                                   |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Grounding      | The model links novel symbols to observed entities and their behavior, rather than relying on memorized descriptions. |
| Generalization | It solves previously unseen combinations of learned relationships.                                                    |
| Transfer       | The sister model succeeds without replaying the original learning episodes.                                           |
| Correction     | A verified change propagates to dependent conclusions, while superseded beliefs cease to be treated as current.       |
| Self-knowledge | The model distinguishes established facts from hypotheses and identifies experiments that could resolve uncertainty.  |

The controls are essential. Compare the autosopher transfer against a transcript, a natural-language summary, ordinary RAG and an unstructured vector-memory baseline. Randomize the vocabulary across runs, hold out entire combinations of relationships and require predictions before revealing the outcome.

Otherwise, a seemingly impressive demonstration could simply be a sophisticated lookup table.

### The critical issue: is this actually solving the Chinese room?

The philosophical distinction matters.

Searle's argument concerns whether manipulating formal symbols can, by itself, produce understanding. The related symbol-grounding problem asks how symbols acquire meaning intrinsic to a system rather than receiving their interpretation entirely from an external observer. These questions remain contested in philosophy of mind. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy +1

The autosopher approach gives us a promising way to investigate the issue experimentally.

A system could ground its concepts in its own history of observations, actions and consequences, and maintain the resulting relationships as persistent, inspectable knowledge. A sister model could inherit that knowledge and demonstrate its utility through novel predictions and successful interventions.

That is stronger evidence of operationally grounded understanding than the ability to manipulate language convincingly in an isolated conversation.

It would not, by itself, prove consciousness or conclusively defeat Searle's philosophical argument. A critic could still claim that the entire system is performing formal operations, including its interaction with a simulated environment. But the architectural response would be substantive: the system does not merely manipulate an externally supplied dictionary of symbols; it builds, tests, revises and transfers a model whose representations are causally connected to observed consequences.

That is a testable research programme, even if the ultimate philosophical interpretation remains disputed.

### One further architectural requirement

I would make the meta-transformer's representation of its own knowledge state explicit.

For each important concept, the system should be able to distinguish:

* Grounded: linked to observations or successful interventions.

* Derived: obtained by applying a defined rule to established premises.

* Hypothetical: proposed but not adequately tested.

* Contradicted: inconsistent with newer evidence or a verified constraint.

* Unresolved: insufficient evidence to choose between alternatives.

These should not be mere labels generated in prose. They should be properties enforced by the memory and validation system.

This would let the meta-transformer inspect its own epistemic state, decide where additional evidence is needed, identify dependencies and explain which part of its knowledge supports a particular conclusion. It would also make the knowledge package transferred to a sister model much more useful than an unqualified collection of assertions.

Recent work on continual-learning benchmarks is beginning to evaluate accumulated experience, feedback and persistent memory directly. The 2026 MemoryBench study reports that existing systems still struggle to use accumulated feedback efficiently, while the Titans architecture demonstrates a distinct route toward learning to memorize during inference. Neither resolves the combination proposed here, but both establish relevant research foundations. Proceedings of Machine Learning Research +1

My preferred first milestone would therefore be a two-model experiment in which Model A learns a previously unknown causal world, exports its autosopher, and Model B independently demonstrates, corrects and extends that knowledge. If the results survive the controls above, we would have evidence for a new property of the overall architecture: knowledge that is acquired through experience, represented explicitly, retained beyond a session and inherited by another model as a functioning capability.

That would be a much more compelling demonstration than claiming that a chatbot understands Chinese. It would show precisely what the system learned, how its representations connect to evidence, where its understanding fails, and whether its discoveries genuinely survive the transfer.
